The report nobody trusts

Most small businesses have the numbers somewhere: leads in the CRM, bookings in the calendar, invoices in the accounting tool, ad spend in another login. Getting them into one picture means someone copying, pasting and checking, and by the time the spreadsheet is finished it is describing last week.

A dashboard connected to the live systems removes that work. The numbers are current when you open the page, calculated the same way every time, and visible to the people who need them without asking anyone.

How we build a dashboard

  1. Start with the questions

    What decisions do you make each week, and what number would make them easier? We write those down first.

  2. Find where the data lives

    We list the systems involved, check what can be connected and flag anything stored where it cannot be reached.

  3. Define each number

    Agree exactly how every figure is calculated, so there is one answer to “what counts as a lead”.

  4. Build and connect

    The dashboard, connected to live data, with access limited to the right people.

  5. Check it against reality

    We compare the first results with your own records, fix differences and only then switch off the old spreadsheet.

Dashboards that people actually use

A dashboard is only worth building if it changes a decision. That is why we start from the questions rather than the data, keep each screen to the handful of numbers that matter and agree the definitions before building anything.

We also keep it honest about what the data can and cannot say. If a figure is based on incomplete tracking, the dashboard says so, rather than presenting a precise-looking number that nobody should rely on.

Who this suits

Businesses where the numbers live in too many places.

  • Owners who build a weekly report by hand
  • Service businesses tracking leads, quotes and bookings across several tools
  • Clinics and studios wanting capacity and no-show visibility
  • Teams running on spreadsheets that only one person understands
  • Businesses paying for marketing who cannot tell what it produces

Questions we get asked

What is a business dashboard?

A single screen that shows the numbers you run the business on, such as leads, bookings, revenue and costs, pulled from your live systems so it is always current, instead of assembled by hand.

How much does a custom dashboard cost?

Scope and price are agreed in writing before work starts, and new projects start at $3,000 CAD. The cost depends on how many systems have to be connected and how many numbers you need.

How long does it take?

A focused dashboard typically fits inside 2 to 6 weeks from kickoff. Larger reporting systems are scoped in stages, with the first stage covering the numbers that matter most.

Can it use data from tools I already pay for?

Often, yes. We check which of your tools can be connected and tell you honestly if some data cannot be reached, before you commit to anything.

Can you replace my weekly spreadsheet report?

That is the most common starting point. We build the dashboard, check it against your spreadsheet, and only switch the spreadsheet off once the numbers match.

Who can see the data?

Only the people you choose. Each user is given access to what they need, and we can limit sensitive figures to owners or managers.

Do you also set up Google Analytics and tracking?

Yes. We set up Analytics, Tag Manager and Search Console so calls, form submissions and bookings are counted, and tie them to the dashboard.
